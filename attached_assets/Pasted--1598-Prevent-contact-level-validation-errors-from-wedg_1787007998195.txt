# #1598 — Prevent contact-level validation errors from wedging GHL circuit recovery

## Required behavior

1. Identify the blocking contact by internal ID using a read-only query.
   - Do not expose its email or other PII.
   - Do not edit or archive it as the primary fix.

2. Validate GHL-bound contact data before provider I/O:
   - Include `email` only when it passes the canonical email validator.
   - If a valid phone exists, allow a phone-only GHL payload where supported.
   - If no usable GHL identity fields remain, classify the contact as a terminal entity skip.
   - Do not call GHL for a payload already known to be invalid.

3. Classify known contact-level GHL validation responses separately:
   - A normalized 422 field-validation error such as `email_invalid` is an entity/data-quality failure.
   - It must not increment `consecutiveFailures`.
   - It must not reopen the provider circuit.
   - Do not broadly ignore every 400/422; unknown responses must retain conservative classification.

4. Emit a sanitized audit event:
   - action: `ghl_sync_skipped_invalid_contact`
   - internal contact ID
   - normalized reason code
   - stage
   - retryable=false
   - no email, phone, provider payload, token, or response body

5. Cursor semantics:
   - Advance and persist `halfOpenProbeCursorId` after every terminal success or skip.
   - Preserve that committed cursor when the circuit opens.
   - Do not advance it for a genuine retryable provider failure; that candidate may be retried after recovery.
   - Do not reset to zero on every `CIRCUIT_OPEN`.
   - Wrap to the beginning only after reaching the end of the candidate population.

6. A half-open probe must scan a bounded number of candidates:
   - Continue past identity conflicts and invalid-contact skips.
   - Close the circuit on the first genuine provider success.
   - If the bounded window contains only skips, persist progress and continue from there on the next natural tick.
   - Only genuine provider/auth/rate-limit/network failures may reopen the circuit.

## Tests — fake provider only

1. Lowest-ID contact has an identity conflict → skipped and cursor advances.
2. Next contact has an invalid email but valid phone → invalid email omitted; valid payload proceeds.
3. Contact has no usable identity fields → skipped without provider I/O.
4. Provider returns known email-validation 422 → entity skip; circuit failure count unchanged.
5. Next valid contact succeeds → circuit closes.
6. Unknown 422 remains conservatively classified and does not silently disappear.
7. 401/403, sustained 429, timeout, and 5xx still affect the circuit according to policy.
8. A genuine provider failure does not discard previously committed skip progress.
9. Multiple half-open processes cannot probe concurrently.
10. No test calls a real GHL endpoint.

## Runtime verification

After merge and publish:

- Do not edit/archive the blocking production contact.
- Do not manually reset the circuit or force a sync.
- Observe 2–3 natural ticks.
- Confirm the invalid contact produces a sanitized skip.
- Confirm cursor movement.
- Confirm `consecutiveFailures` does not increase for that skip.
- Confirm a later valid contact produces `ghl_sync_success`/`ghl_sync_completed`.
- Confirm the circuit closes naturally.
- Keep global outbound pause active and confirm zero outbound messages.
