For Florida auto, med spa, and medical, I’d run three separate plays, not one generic campaign.

The winning pattern is:

1. Build a tight list
2. Use cold call + email + drop-in visits
3. Lead with a problem that matters to that vertical
4. Book a statement review or rate/compliance review

For these niches, ads are secondary. They can help later, but the best early results usually come from targeted outbound plus referral partners.

1) Florida auto

This is probably the easiest of the three to prospect by outbound because owners and GMs are used to vendor calls.

Florida’s Motor Vehicle Repair Act covers a wide range of repair businesses and requires repair shops working on others’ vehicles to register with FDACS; the state also emphasizes estimate/invoice rules and limits on charging for unauthorized repairs. That means these shops already live in an environment where documentation, tickets, and dispute prevention matter.  ￼

Best targets
	•	Independent auto repair shops
	•	Tire and wheel shops
	•	Collision/body shops
	•	Transmission and brake shops
	•	Used car dealers with service departments
	•	Specialty shops: European, diesel, performance, A/C

Best offer angle

Lead with:
	•	lowering effective processing cost
	•	cleaner invoicing / POS fit
	•	text-to-pay or mobile pay
	•	financing options for big repair tickets
	•	chargeback reduction
	•	better support than their current processor

Outreach that works

Cold call + email + in-person is strongest here.

Call script opener:

“We work with Florida repair shops that do larger tickets and get crushed on card fees. We’ve been helping owners lower cost and make front-counter payments smoother. Who handles merchant services there?”

Follow-up email:

Subject: Quick question on card fees at {{shop name}}

Hi {{first name}},
We help Florida repair shops lower card cost and clean up front-counter payment flow, especially on larger repair tickets.

Usually we find one of three issues:
	•	pricing is too high
	•	terminals/POS are clunky
	•	no good process for big-ticket payments or disputes

Worth a quick 10-minute review of your current setup?

– {{name}}

Best partner channels
	•	shop management software resellers
	•	local accountants/bookkeepers
	•	auto shop consultants
	•	equipment/tool reps
	•	local business bankers
	•	dealership and repair associations

Best prospecting list filters
	•	4.3+ Google rating
	•	20+ reviews
	•	independent owner-operated
	•	clear service menu on website
	•	multiple bays or multiple locations
	•	“financing available” or “fleet accounts” on site

Those signals usually indicate a real operator with volume.

2) Florida med spas

This is a great niche, but you need to sound more consultative and credible.

Florida’s health-care licensing system is run through the Department of Health’s Division of Medical Quality Assurance, which regulates practitioners and facilities; most health care licensees and facilities must renew periodically, and recent Florida changes added fingerprint/background-screening requirements for many initial applicants starting July 1, 2025.  ￼

Best targets
	•	single-location med spas
	•	aesthetic clinics doing injectables, laser, body contouring
	•	owner-operator nurse practitioner / physician-led groups
	•	2–5 location groups expanding fast

Best offer angle

Don’t lead with “cheap rates.” Lead with:
	•	recurring billing / memberships
	•	deposits and cancellation protection
	•	card-on-file workflows
	•	patient financing
	•	online checkout links
	•	support for higher-ticket aesthetic procedures
	•	clean reporting for front desk + owner

These businesses care about conversion, no-shows, recurring revenue, and customer experience as much as price.

Outreach that works

For med spas, email + LinkedIn + call usually works better than pure cold calling.

Call opener:

“We work with Florida med spas on memberships, card-on-file, and higher-ticket payment flow. Usually the owner or practice manager handles this. Who would that be?”

Email angle:

Subject: Question about payments at {{med spa name}}

Hi {{first name}},
We help Florida med spas improve payment flow for memberships, packages, deposits, and larger-ticket treatments.

Most groups we talk to are dealing with one of these:
	•	no-show leakage
	•	weak card-on-file process
	•	clunky membership billing
	•	paying too much on processing

Open to a quick review of your current setup?

– {{name}}

Best partner channels
	•	aesthetic device reps
	•	EMR / practice software consultants
	•	med spa coaches / consultants
	•	CPAs focused on aesthetic practices
	•	healthcare attorneys / formation advisors
	•	marketing agencies serving med spas

List-building filters
	•	website offers memberships or treatment packages
	•	online booking enabled
	•	active Instagram presence
	•	multiple providers
	•	50+ Google reviews
	•	Botox, fillers, laser, weight-loss, body-sculpting services listed

Those usually correlate with stronger marketing, higher card usage, and better merchant opportunity.

Important note

With med spas, be careful about sounding like a commodity processor. Owners hear that constantly. Position as:
	•	revenue workflow
	•	payment experience
	•	financing / recurring revenue
	•	support

That framing gets more meetings.

3) Florida medical businesses

This bucket is broad, so I’d split it into:
	•	private practices
	•	dental
	•	chiropractic/PT
	•	urgent care
	•	specialty clinics
	•	multi-location groups

Healthcare businesses also operate under HIPAA rules, and HHS provides guidance for covered entities and business associates. CMS also maintains HIPAA Administrative Simplification standards for health care payment and remittance advice / EFT transactions.  ￼

Best targets

Start with the easiest owner-access niches:
	•	dental
	•	chiropractic
	•	physical therapy
	•	optometry
	•	podiatry
	•	dermatology
	•	urgent care
	•	behavioral health private-pay clinics

Harder:
	•	large hospital-owned groups
	•	heavily centralized networks
	•	practices locked into enterprise systems

Best offer angle

Lead with:
	•	patient collections
	•	text-to-pay
	•	card-on-file
	•	recurring payment plans
	•	front-desk speed
	•	patient financing
	•	cleaner reconciliation
	•	security/compliance awareness

Outreach that works

For medical, email first, call second is often better than walking in cold.

Gatekeeper-friendly script:

“We help Florida medical practices improve patient payment flow — things like text-to-pay, payment plans, and front-desk collections. Who handles payment systems or merchant services there?”

Owner/admin email:

Subject: Patient payment question for {{practice name}}

Hi {{first name}},
We work with Florida practices that want to improve patient collections without making the front desk process harder.

Usually the issues are:
	•	too much manual collection work
	•	poor text-to-pay / card-on-file tools
	•	lack of patient payment plans
	•	merchant pricing that hasn’t been reviewed in years

Would a quick review be worthwhile?

– {{name}}

Best partner channels
	•	healthcare CPAs
	•	EMR/practice management consultants
	•	medical office consultants
	•	healthcare attorneys
	•	outsourced billing companies
	•	local medical associations

Auto

cold call + in-person + email

	•	fast qualification

Med spa

Best channel mix:
email + call + Instagram/LinkedIn + referrals

Why:

	•	consultative sale
	•	better response to polished messaging

Medical

Best channel mix:
email + call + partner referrals

Why:

	•	trust/compliance matter more
	•	warm introductions help a lot

The actual offer to book meetings

Don’t ask for a generic meeting. Offer one of these:
	•	Merchant statement review
	•	Payment workflow review
	•	Membership / recurring billing review for med spas
	•	Patient collections review for medical
	•	Large-ticket payment review for auto

That makes the conversation feel concrete.

⸻

One compliance point to keep straight

If you talk about surcharging in Florida, be precise. Florida requires disclosure of credit card surcharges, and card-brand rules generally require notice, signage, and receipt disclosure; Visa says surcharging applies to credit transactions, not debit/prepaid, and requires 30 days’ notice to the acquirer.  ￼

That matters because many merchants have heard half-true versions of surcharge rules.
