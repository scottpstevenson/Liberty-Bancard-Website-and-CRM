What the 2,520 “no recipe candidate” failures really are
They are not 2,520 separate recipe failures.

They are:

630 unique test contexts

And those 630 contexts are exactly all of the corpus’s allergen-exclusion cases:

The runner is taking each corpus row’s exclusions field and treating it as a real user’s allergy profile, then trying to solve an entire 4–5 meal day.

That is the mistake.

The Authority corpus row is a targeted coverage scenario for one meal family—for example, “prove the Pre-Workout family behaves correctly for a vegetarian user avoiding sesame.” It is not a claim that the library can build an entire daily plan across Breakfast, Lunch, Pre-Workout, Post-Workout, and Dinner for that exact exclusion combination.

So the runner is asking the wrong question:

“Can every slot in an entire day be solved for this one family-level exclusion test?”

Instead of the question it should ask:

“Can the specific family this corpus row represents be safely and correctly evaluated for this exclusion test?”

That is why you see outputs like every slot—Breakfast, Lunch, Pre-, Post-, Dinner—rejected for allergen_exclusion. It does not mean every recipe contains sesame. It means the harness used a narrow family-level safety test as though it were a full-day user profile.
